# OpenShiksha Daily Plan — 2026-06-09

## A. Today's objective

Promote a new top initiative — **[AI Surface Activation](../initiatives/ai-surface-activation.md)**
— and ship its first batch: light up the AI value the backend already has but
the product can't reach. The most important outcome: **students can get an AI
explanation of their graded answers** (first consumer of `/ai/explanations/`),
**teachers can see class-level misconception clusters** (first consumer of
`/ai/misconception-clusters/`), and the student dashboard's AI panels stop
having inert/inconsistent edges.

## B. Why this matters

The board emptied this morning: Authoring Integrity & Versioning closed
(Phase 3, [#279](https://github.com/openshiksha/openshiksha/pull/279)–[#281](https://github.com/openshiksha/openshiksha/pull/281)),
Teacher Workspace closed with TW-2, and every other initiative is Done or
Paused — STATUS.md explicitly said "no unblocked next bet until a new
initiative is promoted."

The strongest candidate came from the repo's own audit trail. The 2026-06-09
entry in [`docs/ai-features/polish-backlog.md`](../ai-features/polish-backlog.md)
found that **four fully-built, tested AI endpoint groups have zero consumers in
`frontend_modern`** (verified again today by grep):

- `/ai/explanations/` — natural-language answer explanations (generate + poll)
- `/ai/misconception-clusters/` — class-level misconception insight for teachers
- `/ai/assignment-drafts/` — AI-assembled draft assignments (generate/approve/dismiss)
- `/ai/open-rubrics/` + `/ai/open-grades/` — open-response grading

That's finished, tested product value sitting dark — activation is cheaper and
higher-leverage than any new build. The same audit listed live-surface gaps
that make perfect batch-fillers: recommendation rows that can't be clicked
(`problem_set`/`chapter` on the payload, unused), `DueForReviewPanel` still has
the render-`null` pop-in that [#283](https://github.com/openshiksha/openshiksha/pull/283)
just fixed on its sibling panel, and the SRS drill's "Review again" double-runs
the SM-2 scheduler in one sitting.

Versus the other backlog candidates: the AI tutor branch needs a risky ~5,000-line
rebase; mobile-shell/PWA and the full a11y audit are vaguer bets. Activation is
concrete, low-risk, and compounds with the AI-polish convention (badges, stub
transparency) established across #283 and earlier polish runs.

### ASA-3 — SRS drill repeat-review guard *(small)*

- **Files:** [SRSDrillPage.tsx](../../frontend_modern/src/features/student/SRSDrillPage.tsx)
  (edit), its test file (extend or create).
- **What:** After submitting a drill, the result screen's "Review again"
  (line ~115) resets `answers`/`result` and lets the student resubmit, which
  calls `markReviewed` again and **double-moves the SM-2 interval in one
  sitting** (flagged in the polish-backlog audit). Fix frontend-first:
  1. Track `hasReviewedThisVisit` (set on first successful `markReviewed`).
  2. Second pass: button copy becomes "Practice again", with a sub-line
     "Extra practice won't change your review schedule." On completing the
     second pass, grade locally (the page already computes correctness for
     display) and **skip the `markReviewed` mutation**; show the result screen
     with a "practice round" label instead of the schedule update.
  3. Note in the change doc that a backend idempotency guard (reject/no-op a
     second review of the same entry on the same day) is ASA-9 in the backlog —
     defence-in-depth, not needed for this PR.
- **Verify:** Vitest: after first submit, second completion does not call the
  mark-reviewed mutation (assert via mocked hook) and shows practice-round copy.
- **Risk:** low; the only subtlety is keeping the first-pass behaviour
  byte-identical. The state flag is page-local, resets on navigation — that's
  correct (a *later* same-day visit is still guarded only once ASA-9 lands;
  acceptable, documented).

## F. Legacy analysis & improvement opportunities

The legacy Django 1.11 app had **none of these AI capabilities** — there is no
legacy reference to port for ASA-4/5; both are *New* by classification. The
relevant comparison is internal consistency:

- **Legacy feedback loop:** after grading, students saw marks only; no
  explanation of *why* an answer was wrong. ASA-4 turns the existing grading
  surface into a teaching moment — this is the "improve, don't just migrate"
  thesis applied to the platform's core loop.
- **Legacy teacher insight:** aggregate scores per class (edge app), nothing
  diagnostic. Misconception clusters (ASA-5) give teachers the *why* behind
  low scores, which legacy never attempted.
- **What legacy got right that we keep:** insight must lead to action in-flow
  (legacy's remedial auto-creation). ASA-2 applies the same principle to
  recommendations — an insight row becomes a one-click practice session.
